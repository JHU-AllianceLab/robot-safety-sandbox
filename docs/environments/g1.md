# G1 stabilize and locomotion

A Unitree G1 humanoid must **stay on its feet** — and, in the locomotion variant,
**keep walking at a commanded velocity** — on flat ground against a worst-case
torso force. It is the humanoid twin of
[Go2 stabilize / locomotion](go2-stabilize.md): same "no-machinery" shape (flat
terrain, stock spawns, no curriculum, no staged pipeline, task-local margins),
29 actuated joints instead of 12.

## Status and requirements

| | |
|---|---|
| Maturity | **Experimental** — new. The environment is verified end to end (builds, steps, margins and terminations agree); **no training result is reported yet.** |
| Requires | stock mjlab sim stack (no fork); NVIDIA GPU |
| Adversary | supported — a torso **wrench** (`g1_stabilize`, `g1_locomote`) or the **arm joints** (`g1_stabilize_arm_adv`, `g1_locomote_arm_adv`) |
| Recommended config | `configs/g1_stabilize_reachavoidppo1p.yaml` (start here) · `configs/g1_locomote_reachavoidppo1p.yaml` · `configs/g1_walker_flat.yaml` (task policy) · `configs/g1_walker_flat_fast.yaml` (faster task policy) |

The G1 robot asset is **stock mjlab** (`mjlab.asset_zoo.robots.get_g1_robot_cfg`)

!!! tip "Which one do you want?"
    If the goal is **a humanoid that walks**, train `g1_walker_flat` first: it is
    the only G1 task with a dense gait reward (velocity tracking, posture,
    foot-gait, clearance, slip). The two reach-avoid tasks reward the safety
    margin `g` and nothing else — they answer *"from here, can it avoid falling
    while doing the task?"*, which is a certificate, not a gait. The three share
    one env cfg, so their observation (98) and action (29) spaces match and a
    reach-avoid run can warm-start from the walker with `--load` (keep `net:`
    equal; the trainer reports how many tensors transferred and fails loud if
    none did).

## What success and failure mean

- **Failure** (`g < 0`): the robot falls — pelvis below the floor band, torso
  tilted past 50°, or **any non-foot body touching the ground** (a knee, a hand,
  the torso). The episode terminates.
- **Success** (`l ≥ 0`): in the target stance set (`g1_stabilize`) or tracking
  the commanded velocity within 0.5 m/s (`g1_locomote`).

## Observation and action spaces

- **Observations**: 98-dim proprioception — base angular velocity, projected
  gravity, the twist command, a gait-phase clock, joint positions and velocities,
  and the previous action. No height scan (flat ground). The cfg also emits a
  larger `critic` group (base linear velocity, foot height / air time / contact /
  forces) for asymmetric setups.
- **Action**: 29 joint targets (`ctrl_dim=29`) — 12 leg, 3 waist, 14 arm.
- **Adversary action** (`--adversary`): a 3-dim wrench on `torso_link`, scaled by
  the training-time magnitude; `--dstb-scale` sweeps it at eval.

!!! warning "Action scaling differs from the Go2 — and it matters at eval"
    mjlab's G1 action term carries a **per-joint** scale (`G1_ACTION_SCALE`,
    0.25·effort/stiffness — 0.35 rad/unit at a knee, 0.55 at a hip pitch), so the
    tasks register `ctrl_gain=1.0` and let the mjlab scale be the joint authority,
    and `action_bound=3.0` so the bound does not cap the stride (the default 1.0
    caps a knee at 0.35 rad and produces a shuffle — T006). `examples/eval.py`
    clamps a nominal policy to `--action-bound`, **which defaults to 1.0**: pass
    `--action-bound 3.0` for these tasks or you evaluate a different policy than
    you trained.

## Margins

Defined task-locally in
`robot_safety_sandbox/envs/g1_stabilize/env_cfg.py` (~60 lines), not composed
from `margins.py`. Both are clamped to ±3.

- **`g`** (safety) = `min` of three normalized terms: pelvis height above 0.45 m,
  torso tilt within 50° of vertical, and non-foot ground-contact force under
  10 N. It rides on the reward channel and the env terminates on `g < 0`.
- **`l`** (target):
    - `g1_stabilize` — pelvis inside a 0.55–0.85 m band, within 25° of upright,
      and both velocities small (each term divided by its own tolerance, so no
      one dimension dominates the `min`).
    - `g1_locomote` — planar body-frame velocity within `LOCO_VEL_TOL` (0.5 m/s)
      of the command, normalized by that tolerance so `l = 1` at perfect tracking
      — the same O(1) scale as `g`, which matters because the reach-avoid backup
      compares them directly in `min(g, max(l, γV'))`.

**Why not the Go2's margin?** The quadruped's `g` is the minimum height of the
eight trunk corners: a Go2 trunk is a box near the ground and a fall *is* the box
touching it. A G1 is 1.3 m of articulated linkage whose pelvis stays high in
poses that are already a fall — a kneel, a hand plant — so height alone does not
decide it. The contact term is what catches those, and it reads the **same
sensor** as the env's `illegal_contact` termination, so `g < 0` whenever the
episode ends on a fall.

See [margins](../concepts/margins.md).

## The arm adversary

`g1_stabilize_arm_adv` and `g1_locomote_arm_adv` are the same two problems with a
different adversary. Instead of a 3-dim wrench on `torso_link`, the disturbance
player **owns 14 of the robot's own actuators**:

| Action dims | Joints | Player |
|---|---|---|
| `0–11` | hips, knees, ankles | control |
| `12–14` | waist yaw / roll / pitch | control |
| `15–28` | shoulders, elbows, wrists | **disturbance** |

A G1's arms are roughly 10 kg at the end of a long lever. Swung worst-case they
are a far more expressive attack than a bounded force, and one a deployed robot
can actually suffer: a mistrained or hijacked arm controller, a carried load that
swings, an arm policy trained for some other task running alongside the gait.
This is also the direct answer to the last bullet under
[Known limitations](#known-limitations) — the arms stop being unconstrained
scenery and become the thing you are being robustified against.

### The actor keeps its full 29-dim head

The bridge (`dstb_mode="joint"`) **overwrites** dims 15–28 of the actor's action
with the adversary's command rather than shrinking the actor to 15 outputs. The
actor's arm outputs are simply discarded. This is deliberate: it means a trained
`g1_stabilize` / `g1_stabilize_adv` checkpoint warm-starts **whole** — trunk,
action head and `log_std` — which a 15-wide actor could not, and that transfer is
the point of the task.

The actor is not blind to the attack: mjlab's `last_action` observation term
reads the vector the bridge actually submitted, so next step's observation
carries the *adversary's* arm command, not the actor's discarded one.

### Why the self-collision penalty is not in `g`

The adversary has to be stopped from winning the degenerate way — driving the
hands through the torso and the thighs, where the contact solver hands it
impulses no physical arm could produce. But the two-player learners are
**zero-sum**: `ppo_2p` negates the disturbance advantage on the *same* targets the
control player was scored on, and `sac_2p` shares one joint-action critic. There
is one reward channel, so anything folded into `g` or into the shared `l` is
something the *minimizing* player wants **more** of. A self-collision term in `g`
would pay the adversary to smash its own arms into the torso.

The penalty is delivered instead as a **disturbance-phase reach margin**.
`callbacks.DstbPhaseCallback` reads the learner's own `_phase()` at each rollout
start and stamps it on the env as `_zoo_dstb_phase`; while the disturbance player
is collecting, the margin function returns

$$l_\text{dstb} = l + \text{frac}\cdot(\text{CLAMP} - l), \qquad
  \text{frac} = \min\!\left(\frac{\text{arm self-contact force}}{10\,\text{N}}, 1\right)$$

i.e. `l` interpolated toward the `+3` ceiling as the arms load up against the
body. The disturbance minimizes `min(g, max(l, γV'))`, so a spike in `l` is a
spike in the value it is trying to push down, and it learns to keep the arms
clear. Because it is a lerp rather than a `max`, an adversary that is *winning*
(`l` deeply negative) still gets a gradient out of a grazing contact instead of a
flat plateau.

Two properties this preserves, both pinned by `tests/test_g1_arm_adversary.py`:

- **`g` is never shaped.** It stays the shared physical safety margin, so the
  failure counters, the league's `_ever_gneg` bookkeeping and the certificate all
  keep their meaning.
- **At zero self-contact `l_dstb == l` exactly**, bit for bit. So the control
  player's problem — and every 1P run of these tasks — is identical to
  `g1_stabilize`'s, which is what makes the 1P run an honest baseline.

The contact signal is a dedicated `arm_self_collision` sensor: the eight arm
collision geoms against the robot subtree. It is deliberately *not* the cfg's
existing `self_collision` sensor, which also fires on thigh-against-thigh — that
is the actor's doing and must not be charged to the adversary. The secondary is a
**subtree** rather than a geom regex because mjlab's `secondary_policy` would
otherwise collapse a multi-match pattern to one geom (the sensor then reads zero
forever, which looks exactly like a well-behaved adversary) or drop the filter
entirely and also catch arm-versus-*terrain*, charging the adversary for the fall
it is being trained to cause.

### The authority curriculum

There is no external wrench here, so `--force-max` and the per-env force
curriculum are not read at all — the trainer skips their callbacks and says so.
The knob is `--dstb-gain`, the adversary's joint authority, ramped from
`--dstb-gain-init` over `--force-ramp-frac` of the run by
`callbacks.DstbGainRampCallback`. `1.0` is exactly the per-joint authority the
actor has, since mjlab's per-joint `G1_ACTION_SCALE` applies to both players
alike. Same reason as the force ramp for starting low: an adversary at full
authority from step 0 just throws the robot down every episode, the actor never
sees a survivable state, and the game freezes with no gradient.

On top of the ramp sits a **per-env survival curriculum** — the joint-mode twin
of the wrench's `force_scale`. Each env slot carries its own authority multiplier
`s_i ∈ [--dstb-scale-floor, 1]` (starting at `--dstb-scale-init`); when that env's
episode survives to the timeout `s_i += 0.05`, when it falls `s_i -= 0.05`. The
arm command is `dstb_gain(t) × s_i × a_dstb`, so the adversary weakens exactly in
the envs where it is winning and each env settles near the arm strength the robot
survives about half the time. The adversary cannot observe `s_i`; to it, the same
command simply lands harder in some envs than others.

Watch two scalars: `game/dstb_scale_mean` climbing to 1.0 means the actor has
caught up with full-authority arms; `game/dstb_scale_at_floor` near 1.0 means it
is losing even at the floor — lower `--dstb-scale-floor`. The guarantee a run
earns is against the arm strengths it actually trained at, so if the mean
settles below 1.0 that number *is* the result. It is on by default;
`--no-dstb-per-env-scale` reproduces the global-ramp-only behavior of the first
arm run, whose training failure rate sat near 0.9 for the whole second half once
the ramp saturated.

!!! warning "Use the default `end_criterion="failure"`"
    Under `end_criterion="reach-avoid"` a shaped `l` would end the episode on the
    *adversary's* self-collision, which is nonsense. The shaping is skipped
    outright in that mode rather than silently corrupting the terminations, so
    the run is valid — but the adversary is then unpenalized, which is not the
    task you meant to train.

## Random arms

`g1_stabilize_arm_random` and `g1_locomote_arm_random` are the single-player
stepping stone between the parked-arm tasks and the arm adversary. The joint
split is the arm adversary's (the actor keeps dims 0..14, dims 15..28 are
overwritten), but the arm commands come from a **smooth random process** in the
bridge (`joint_noise.SmoothJointNoise`), not from a second player:

- each arm joint of each env glides between random waypoints on a
  **minimum-jerk** profile (`s(u) = 10u³ − 15u⁴ + 6u⁵`), one segment every
  0.5–2 s, so the command is C2: no steps, no velocity kinks. Joints and envs are
  independent;
- every episode starts from the default pose, where the reset leaves the arms;
- the waypoints span the full `[-1, 1]` on every joint **except the shoulder
  roll, which only swings outward**. Measured with the arm adversary's
  `arm_self_collision` sensor: with a symmetric range the arms were on the
  torso in 16% of upright steps at magnitude 2, nearly all of it from inward
  roll; one-sided roll brings that to 0.5% (2% at magnitude 3).

Margins, cfg, obs and the 29-wide head are exactly `g1_stabilize`'s (the
`last_action` obs carries the arm command about to be applied, as it does under
the adversary), so a checkpoint warm-starts from `g1_stabilize` and into
`g1_stabilize_arm_adv` whole.

**The magnitude curriculum** reuses the arm adversary's two levers unchanged,
because the bridge applies the random command through the same code path:
`dstb_gain` is the waypoint half-width in action units (2.0 registered =
±0.88 rad at the shoulders and elbows), ramped from `--dstb-gain-init` over
`--force-ramp-frac` of the run, times the per-env survival scale `s_i`. The
segment durations are fixed, so a larger magnitude is also a faster swing. Watch
`game/dstb_gain`, `game/dstb_scale_mean` and `game/dstb_scale_at_floor`, as
above. Only the PPO trainer wires this; the SAC trainer runs the arms at full
magnitude from step 0 and says so.

## Fall recovery

`g1_recover` and `g1_recover_arm_random` train the **fallback** a G1 safety
filter hands control to. They are g1_stabilize's env (flat ground, zero
command, same margins and head) under the **avoid** backup, `V = min(g, γV')`:
V > 0 reads "will not fall", and the robot may keep jogging. What is new is
**where episodes start**: a mixture over a harvested start-state bank.

| Source | Share | What it is | Sorted by (easy → hard) |
|---|---|---|---|
| `nominal` | 15% | the default stance at rest | — |
| `clean` | 35% | the walker mid-gait, undisturbed | speed |
| `pushed` | 15% | the walker 20–300 ms after a random push, and it survived | push size |
| `fell` | 20% | walker rows the walker then **fell** from within 2 s | time-to-fall, longest first |
| `synthetic` | 15% | stance + random tilt (≤25°), twist (≤1.5 m/s), joint offsets | perturbation scale |

- **Harvest the bank** with `examples/harvest_g1_recover_states.py --walker
  <walker zip>` (writes `data/g1_recover_states.pt`; override with
  `env_overrides: {dataset: ...}` or `$SAFE_ZOO_G1_RECOVER_DATASET`). States are
  stored yaw-free; the spawn re-draws heading and planar offset. The script
  replays rows into the env they came from and prints replay-vs-label agreement
  next to a replay-vs-replay noise floor. Synthetic rows are height-aligned by
  forward kinematics so the lowest sole point sits on the ground.
- **The first observation is a real handover**: the spawn restores the
  walker's last action into `last_action` (seeded from the event's `reset()`
  hook, after mjlab clears the action buffer). In `g1_recover_arm_random` the
  arms glide from the walker's arm command into the random motion instead of
  snapping to the default pose.
- **Per-env, per-source curriculum**: each env holds a level 0..5 per source;
  a level picks a window of that source's sorted rows, a timeout promotes it, a
  fall demotes it. Watch `Curriculum/recover/surv_<source>`,
  `lvl_<source>`, `top_<source>` and `surv_v<lo>_<hi>` (by spawn speed).
- Episodes are 8 s (recovery takes 1–2 s; timeouts are not bootstrapped).
- With no bank on disk the spawn warns and falls back to the nominal stance.
- `g1_recover_arm_random_wrench` adds a **learned torso wrench** on top of the
  random arms (`--adversary` → `SafetyPPO2P`). The two disturbances use separate
  channels: the arms keep their joint slice and magnitude knobs (`dstb_gain`,
  `dstb_scale`), while the adversary picks a 3-dim force direction applied at
  `force_max` × the per-env `force_scale`, as on `g1_stabilize`. Both curricula
  run. Watch `game/force_scale_mean` / `game/force_scale_at_floor` for the wrench.
- `g1_recover_wrench` is the same learned torso wrench on `g1_recover` (all 29
  joints, no random arms), so a `g1_recover` checkpoint warm-starts it whole
  (`configs/g1_recover_wrench_safetyppo2p.yaml`).
- `g1_recover_lafan`, `g1_recover_lafan_ra` and `g1_recover_lafan_ra_loose`
  are the tasks of the [LAFAN-recovery experiments](../experiments/g1-lafan-recovery.md)
  (configs from `configs/sweeps/lafan_recovery.yaml` via `examples/make_sweep.py`).

## Reach-avoid-stay to a waist goal

The goal is a waist posture and a body-frame velocity: waist roll and pitch
targets (the `waist_target` command, one draw per episode) and (vx, vy, wz) (the
`twist` command, pinned to zero for now). **Reach-avoid-stay** here means: get
to the goal set without falling, then remain inside it without falling. A
reach-avoid value certifies only the first half, so the problem is split
into two policies, trained one after the other and switched at deploy time.

| | `g1_waist_stay_arm_random` | `g1_waist_reach_arm_random` |
|---|---|---|
| backup | avoid, `V = min(h, γV')` | reach-avoid, `V = min(g, max(l, γV'))` |
| constraint | `h = min(g, soft(l_box))`: safe **and** in the goal set | `g` |
| target | — | `l = min(l_box, V_stay)`, V_stay from the **frozen** stay twin |
| spawn | waist within 0.05 rad of the goal, base moving at ≤ 0.05, legs in a random stance | stock: at rest in the default pose |
| train | first | second (loads the stay run) |

- **`g`** is g1_stabilize's fall margin, unchanged (pelvis height ≥ 0.45 m,
  torso tilt ≤ 50°, no non-foot ground contact).
- **`l_box`** is the min over five terms, each normalized by its tolerance:
  `|q_waist_roll − roll*|` and `|q_waist_pitch − pitch*|` within **0.1 rad**,
  `|v_x − vx*|`, `|v_y − vy*|` (pelvis, body frame) and `|w_z − wz*|` within
  **0.1** (m/s, rad/s). vz, wx and wy are not part of the goal.
- **Stay spawn stance.** Instead of the one knees-bent pose, each stay episode
  starts the legs in a stance drawn from a bank (`envs/g1_waist/stance.py`):
  pelvis height −0.12…+0.02 m from nominal, pelvis roll ±0.15 and pitch ±0.2
  rad, pelvis ±0.04 m over the feet, stance width −0.03…+0.12 m, feet
  staggered fore-aft by up to ±0.12 m, toes ±0.25 rad. The leg joints are solved by IK so both
  feet are flat on the floor, every joint is inside its soft limits, and the
  CoM is ≥ 2 cm inside the support polygon. `spawn_stance_scale` scales every
  range (0 = the stock pose).
- **Why the stay constraint reshapes `l_box`.** The avoid value is the worst
  `h` along the trajectory, and a fall ends the trajectory. With a raw `l_box`,
  a fall (`g` ≈ −0.84 when `fell_over` fires at 70°) scored better than
  drifting more than 0.185 out of the box later, and the first stay run learned
  to fall on purpose within ~0.5 s. So a failure termination is valued at −3
  instead of the zoo-wide −0.1, and `l_box` is compressed below zero: `l` for
  `l ≥ 0`, `0.5·asinh(l/0.5)` below. The goal set and the margin near its edge
  are unchanged, an upright robot stays well above −3 (−1.75 with the waist at
  the far stop), and a bigger violation still scores worse than a smaller one.
  A flat tail (`0.5·tanh(l/0.5)`, the second run) lost that last property, and
  the policy parked both waist joints at one stop whatever the goal.
- **Why the waist is stiffer than stock.** The upper body is ~15 kg of 33.
  With the pelvis level, gravity's torque at waist roll and pitch grows ~20 Nm
  per rad of lean, cancelling ~70% of mjlab's waist stiffness (28.5 Nm/rad): a
  target of ±0.17 rad already put roll on its ±0.52 stop, so ~80% of the action
  range only pressed the joint into its stop. Both waist tasks run waist roll
  and pitch at 3× the stock kp and kd and an action scale of 0.2 rad/unit (stock
  0.4386). **Sim-to-real:** match this to the real robot's waist gains.
- **Why the reach target is V_stay, not the box.** A state inside the box that
  is already leaving it is not one the stay policy can hold. The reach twin is
  trained to arrive where `V_stay > 0`. The min with `l_box` keeps a V_stay
  that overestimates far from the goal (states its training never visited)
  from counting as arrival there. V_stay is read inside the margin from the
  current state's actor observation, rebuilt noise-free from the observation
  terms (`envs/g1_waist/stay_value.py`).
- **Observation**: g1_stabilize's actor group plus `base_lin_vel` (3, noise
  ±0.05 m/s) and `waist_target` (2), appended at the end: 103 wide. The two
  tasks share the layout, so the reach run warm-starts whole from the stay run.
  A `--load` from a g1_stabilize* run transfers every layer except the input one.
- **Goal curriculum**: goals are uniform in ±`scale` × 0.4 rad (the joints stop
  at ±0.52). `scale` ramps linearly from `--target-scale-init` (0) to 1 over
  `--target-ramp-frac` (0.5) of the run. It is ramped only in the training env:
  eval, play and the training video use the full range. Watch
  `curriculum/waist_target_scale` and `Metrics/waist_target/error_waist`.
- **Arms**: random, as in `g1_stabilize_arm_random`, but at **half** its
  magnitude (`dstb_gain` 1.0: waypoints up to ±0.44 rad at the shoulders and
  elbows), with the same 0 → 1 ramp and per-env survival scale.
- **The waist has to be commanded past its goal.** The waist position gain is
  28.5 N·m/rad against ~15 kg above the joint, which leaves ~0.5 rad of
  steady-state error at a 0.4 rad lean. So holding that goal takes a waist
  action of ~2 (of the ±3 bound). The configs therefore carry no action-
  magnitude penalty.
- `safety/margin_*` on the stay run read `h`, so they include leaving the goal
  set. `safety/failure_*` counts falls only (terminations). On the reach run,
  `safety/reach_rate` and `safety/time_to_reach_s` track the safe reach of
  `l = min(l_box, V_stay) ≥ 0` (logged as `env/safety/*`). That is arrival
  somewhere the stay policy certifies. The deploy switch hands over only at
  `V_stay > 0.1`, which is stricter.

**Deploying the pair** (`filters/switch.py`, `ReachAvoidStayFilter`): each phase
is a value filter, and a latched per-env mode switches between them on the stay
certificate. REACH → STAY when `V_stay > --stay-eps-in` (0.1). STAY → REACH when
`V_stay ≤ --stay-eps-out` (0.0). Every episode starts in REACH. The evaluation
adds `ras_reached_rate`, `ras_time_to_stay_s`, `ras_stay_frac`,
`ras_leaves_per_reach`, and `ras_hold_rate`. `ras_hold_rate` is the fraction of
STAY steps on which `h ≥ 0` actually held, measured on the live state.

## Training budget and hardware

Measured on an RTX 5080 (16 GB), `g1_locomote`, random actions:

| envs | throughput | GPU used by the sim |
|---|---|---|
| 2048 | ~75k env-steps/s | 0.70 GiB |
| 4096 | ~102k env-steps/s | 2.03 GiB |

So the sim is not the constraint; the budget is. A humanoid gait is expensive —
mjlab's own rsl_rl G1 recipe runs ~3×10⁹ env-steps — and the shipped
`g1_walker_flat` recipe starts at 5×10⁸. The reach-avoid recipes start at
1–2×10⁸; as with every task here, budget by episode-generations rather than
habit (see [PORTING.md](../porting.md)).

## Video clips

The G1 cfg sets a 1280x720 offscreen viewer (mjlab's default is 320x240), following
the same per-task convention as `envs/car_goal/env_cfg.py`. The renderer reads
width and height **once, at env construction**, so this has to live on the cfg —
a callback cannot raise it afterwards the way it can with `max_extra_envs`.

Every clip is written to `<run>/videos/step_<N>.mp4` whether or not wandb is in
use; the three G1 recipes set `video_wandb: false`, so wandb keeps all the scalar
curves and the footage stays on disk.

!!! warning "Check that you are rendering on the GPU"
    MuJoCo binds its GL backend at import. With `MUJOCO_GL` unset it may pick
    GLFW and land on Mesa's **`llvmpipe` software rasterizer**, which renders on
    the CPU — measured on this stack at **454 ms per 720p frame**, so a 20 s clip
    costs 7.6 minutes and 50 clips cost 6.3 hours. On a GPU context the same
    frame is **1.1 ms**, and those 50 clips cost under a minute.

    | backend | `GL_RENDERER` | ms/frame @ 720p | 20 s clip |
    |---|---|---|---|
    | `MUJOCO_GL` unset → GLFW | `llvmpipe` (CPU) | 454 | 7.6 min |
    | `MUJOCO_GL=egl` | integrated GPU | 22.6 | 23 s |
    | `MUJOCO_GL=egl` + NVIDIA vendor lib | `NVIDIA GeForce RTX 5080` | **1.1** | **1.1 s** |

    ```bash
    export MUJOCO_GL=egl
    export __EGL_VENDOR_LIBRARY_FILENAMES=/usr/share/glvnd/egl_vendor.d/10_nvidia.json
    ```

    Verify with `glGetString(GL_RENDERER)` on the live context — if it says
    `llvmpipe`, you are rendering on the CPU. Under software GL the per-frame cost
    is dominated by rasterization, so `--video-herd 0` (one robot instead of the
    8-robot herd) roughly halves it and dropping shadows helps again; on a GPU
    context neither knob is worth touching. The scene rebuild on the CPU side is
    0.9 ms either way — it is never the bottleneck.

## Train

```bash
# the dense-reward task policy — train this first if you want a walking gait
python examples/train.py --config configs/g1_walker_flat.yaml

# the same walker, trained on vx in (-0.5, 1.5) m/s from the first iteration
python examples/train.py --config configs/g1_walker_flat_fast.yaml

# reach-avoid, single-player (PPO): stand
python examples/train.py --config configs/g1_stabilize_reachavoidppo1p.yaml

# reach-avoid, single-player: keep walking at 1 m/s
python examples/train.py --config configs/g1_locomote_reachavoidppo1p.yaml

# two-player (the robustification channel). A G1 is 33 kg vs the Go2's 15, so the
# 50 N default force is a gentle push — raise it.
python examples/train.py --config configs/g1_stabilize_reachavoidppo1p.yaml \
    --adversary --force-max 100

# ARM adversary: the adversary commands the 14 arm joints, the actor the rest.
# Warm-starts WHOLE from the wrench-adversary run above (identical 29-dim head).
python examples/train.py --config configs/g1_stabilize_armadv_reachavoidppo2p.yaml \
    --load runs/g1_stabilize_ppo_adv/g1_stabilize_adv/final_model.zip

# the same task WITHOUT an adversary: arms parked at the default pose. This is
# the baseline the 2P run is measured against, and its margins are bit-identical
# to g1_stabilize's. (`adversary: true` lives in the yaml, so drop the config.)
python examples/train.py --family on_policy --task g1_stabilize_arm_adv \
    --net 512,256,128 --lr 1e-4 --no-adaptive-lr --ent-coef 1e-3

# RANDOM arms, single-player: the arms swing smoothly at random while the actor
# stands; their magnitude ramps 0 -> 2.0 over the first half of the run.
python examples/train.py --config configs/g1_stabilize_armrandom_reachavoidppo1p.yaml

# warm-start a reach-avoid run from the walker (same obs/action spaces)
python examples/train.py --config configs/g1_locomote_reachavoidppo1p.yaml \
    --load runs/g1_walker_flat/final_model.zip

# FALL RECOVERY (avoid-only): harvest the start states from a walker, then train
python examples/harvest_g1_recover_states.py --walker runs/g1_walker_flat_fast/final_model.zip
python examples/train.py --config configs/g1_recover_safetyppo1p.yaml            # all 29 joints
python examples/train.py --config configs/g1_recover_armrandom_safetyppo1p.yaml  # arms random
# ...then the random arms PLUS a learned torso wrench (SafetyPPO2P), warm-started
# from the random-arm run (the `load:` in the yaml)
python examples/train.py --config configs/g1_recover_armrandom_wrench_safetyppo2p.yaml \
    --out runs/g1_recover_armrandom_wrench
# ...or all 29 joints PLUS the wrench, warm-started from the all-joint run
python examples/train.py --config configs/g1_recover_wrench_safetyppo2p.yaml \
    --out runs/g1_recover_wrench

# REACH-AVOID-STAY to a waist goal: the stay twin first, then the reach twin,
# whose target is the stay twin's certified set (env_overrides.stay_run)
python examples/train.py --config configs/g1_waist_stay_armrandom_safetyppo1p.yaml
python examples/train.py --config configs/g1_waist_reach_armrandom_reachavoidppo1p.yaml
```

The commanded speed is an env override, not a trainer flag:

```bash
python examples/train.py --config configs/g1_locomote_reachavoidppo1p.yaml \
    --env-override cmd_vx=0.6
```

## Evaluate

The harness *dictates* the twist command (`--cmd-vx`, default 1.0), overriding
the value the task cfg pins — so pass `--cmd-vx 0` when evaluating the standing
twin, or you are asking a rest-trained policy to walk.

```bash
# the safety policy's own reach / safe rates, unfiltered
python examples/eval.py --task g1_stabilize --cmd-vx 0 \
    --safety-policy runs/g1_stabilize_reachavoidppo1p/final_model.zip --safety-only \
    --no-filter --num-envs 256 --steps 1000 --action-bound 3.0

# filter gauntlet: the walker under a swept adversarial attack
python examples/eval.py --task g1_locomote --adversary \
    --task-policy   runs/g1_walker_flat/final_model.zip \
    --safety-policy runs/g1_stabilize_reachavoidppo1p/final_model.zip \
    --filter value --dstb policy --dstb-scale 0.5 --num-envs 256 --action-bound 3.0

# reach-avoid-stay: the reach twin, then the stay twin once V_stay clears 0.1,
# each driving (--safety-only). Command surgery is off in this mode: the twist
# command is part of the goal. Twins are clamped to the task's ±3 action box.
python examples/eval.py --task g1_waist_reach_arm_random --safety-only \
    --safety-policy runs/g1_waist_reach_arm_random/final_model.zip \
    --stay-policy   runs/g1_waist_stay_arm_random/final_model.zip \
    --num-envs 256 --steps 1000
```

## What is verified today

The environment, not a policy: both tasks build and step on stock mjlab; the
margins are finite and O(1); `g = 1.0` at the spawn stance and reaches the ±3
clamp on a fall; across 518 sampled fall terminations **every one** had `g < 0`
at the terminating step (max −0.84), so the margin's failure set and the env's
termination set agree. The tensor bridge (1P, and 2P with the 32-dim joint
action), the numpy bridge, and the `cmd_vx` env override all round-trip.

For the arm tasks specifically: the 15/14 split is pinned against the compiled
mjlab asset (so an asset reshuffle fails a test rather than handing the adversary
somebody's knee); the adversary's dims drive only the arms and leave the legs at
their commanded values; the `arm_self_collision` sensor fires exactly when the
cfg's whole-body `self_collision` sensor does and reads zero otherwise; `l_dstb`
saturates at `+3` under a slam and is bit-identical to `l` with the arms at rest;
and a 2P `ReachAvoidPPO2P` run trains end to end with the phase flag tracking
safety_sb3's own `phase_is_dstb` exactly and the authority ramping 0.1 → 1.0.

## Known limitations

- **No trained result yet.** Nothing on this page reports a converged policy,
  a reach rate, or a certificate.
- Like `go2_locomote`, `g1_locomote` has no locomotion-specific fallback of its
  own: using the `g1_stabilize` twin as the filter's fallback puts a
  rest-trained policy out of distribution when it is asked to hold a 1 m/s gait.
- `g1_stabilize` spawns *inside* its own target set (as `go2_stabilize` does), so
  the reach-avoid value there means "can hold / return to the stance", not "can
  ever touch it". The task therefore keeps `end_criterion="failure"` and never
  ends an episode on reach. If you want the strict "remain in the stance set
  forever" formulation instead, that is the avoid backup on `min(g, l)` — see the
  `digit_stabilize_stay` discussion in [Digit stabilize](digit.md).
- On `g1_stabilize` / `g1_locomote` the arms are actuated and unconstrained: the
  policy is free to windmill them. That is the mjlab G1 default, and the dense
  `pose` / `angular_momentum` terms are the only thing discouraging it — and the
  reach-avoid tasks do not use those terms at all. The `*_arm_adv` tasks take the
  arms away from the policy entirely and hand them to the adversary.
- The arm adversary's self-collision penalty rides on `l` during disturbance
  rollouts, so a self-collision registers as a "reach" in the league's
  `_episode_success` bookkeeping for those rollouts. The effect is bounded — that
  flag only counts on episodes that timed out with `g` never negative, i.e. ones
  the adversary had already lost, and on `g1_stabilize_arm_adv` the robot spawns
  in its target set so the flag is near-always set anyway. It is worth knowing
  before reading a league table from `g1_locomote_arm_adv`.
- **No trained result for the arm tasks either.** The environment is verified end
  to end (below); no policy is reported.
